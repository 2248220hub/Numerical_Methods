BASICSignals in astrophysics are continuous

  • Gravitational-wave strain h(t) in a laser interferometer; light curves I(t) from Kepler/TESS photometry.
  • Radio sky brightness I(l, m); CCD/CMOS pixel arrays I[i, j] — a spatial sampling of the telescope PSF.
  • CMB time-ordered data d(t) recorded while a telescope scans the sky.

A digital computer has finite memory and finite precision: it cannot store a function, only a list of numbers. We discretise along two orthogonal axes:

\underbrace{\h{xn}{x[n]}=x(\h{n}{n}\h{T}{T})}_{\text{sampling (domain)}},\qquad \underbrace{\h{Qx}{Q(x)}=\h{Delta}{\Delta}\cdot\text{round}(x/\h{Delta}{\Delta})}_{\text{quantisation (range)}},\qquad \h{fs}{f_s}=\frac1{\h{T}{T}} T: sampling interval [s]; fs: sampling rate [Hz]; Δ: quantisation step (the least significant bit, LSB). t = np.arange(N) / fs # sample times nT x = np.sin(2*np.pi*f0*t) # x[n] xq = Delta * np.round(x / Delta) # quantised t = [(n, n = 0, N-1)] / fs x = sin(2*pi*f0*t) xq = Delta * anint(x / Delta)

COREManaging the dynamic range

  • Under-utilisation: a signal using a fraction ρ of full scale has Beff = B + log₂ ρ. A quarter of the range loses 2 bits (12 dB). Fix: analogue gain before the ADC.
  • Clipping: above Vmax the output is flat: a non-linear distortion that creates fake spectral lines (harmonics).
  • DC offset: a unipolar sensor (0–1 V) in a bipolar ADC (±2.5 V) uses at most a fifth of the codes: shift and scale first.

  3. Convolving with a shifted δ shifts: every term gives a copy of X centred at mΩs.

Preventing aliasing

An analogue low-pass anti-aliasing filter must sit before the ADC: it attenuates everything above fs − fmax (what would fold into [0, fmax]) down to the noise floor. Oversampling (fs ≫ 2fmax) relaxes the filter's roll-off.

The DFT only sees N samples and N frequencies fk = k fs/N. A tone exactly on a bin lands in one bin; a tone between bins spreads over all of them (leakage).

Bin spacing Δf = fs/N = 1/Tobs. Zero padding only interpolates the spectrum: it does not improve resolution.

CORETwo steps to a computable transform

  2. Sample the DTFT at ωk = 2πk/N, k = 0 … N−1.
\h{Xk}{X[k]}=\h{sum}{\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}}\h{xn}{x[n]}\,e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{N}kn},\qquad \h{xn}{x[n]}=\frac1{\h{N}{N}}\h{sum}{\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}}\h{Xk}{X[k]}\,e^{j\frac{2\pi}{N}kn} X = np.fft.fft(x) # forward, no 1/N x = np.fft.ifft(X) # inverse carries 1/N call dft(z, Zk, -1) ! dft_direct.f90 call dft(Zk, z2, +1); z2 = z2/N \h{WN}{W_N}=e^{-j2\pi/N},\qquad \h{Xk}{X[k]}=\h{sum}{\sum_n}x[n]\,\h{WN}{W_N}^{kn},\qquad \mathbf X=\mathbf F_N\mathbf x,\quad \mathbf F_N^{-1}=\frac1N\mathbf F_N^* n = np.arange(N); W = np.exp(-2j*np.pi*np.outer(n, n)/N) X = W @ x # O(N^2) do k = 0, N-1 do j = 0, N-1 W(k+1, j+1) = exp(cmplx(0.0_dp, -2*pi*mod(k*j, N)/real(N, dp), dp)) end do end do X = matmul(W, x)

Orthogonality of the basis

  1. \(\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}W_N^{kn}W_N^{-rn}=\sum_n W_N^{(k-r)n}\).
  2. k = r: every term is 1, the sum is N.
  3. k ≠ r: a geometric series, \(\frac{1-W_N^{(k-r)N}}{1-W_N^{k-r}}=\frac{1-1}{1-W_N^{k-r}}=0\). Hence \(\sum_n W^{kn}W^{-rn}=N\delta_{kr}\).

COREPeriodicity, grid and symmetry

  • Implicit periodicity: the DFT treats x[n] and X[k] as one period of periodic sequences; shifts are circular, x[(n−n₀) mod N] ⇔ WNkn₀X[k]; a product of DFTs is a circular convolution (Lecture 9: zero padding).
  • Grid: fk = k fs/N, spacing Δf = fs/N = 1/(NT) = 1/Tobs.
  • Real input: X[k] = X*[(N−k) mod N]; X[0] and (N even) X[N/2] are real. N real numbers ↔ N/2+1 unique bins.
